# MVP Prototype Testing

## Purpose

Before implementing the UQ Club Discovery Hub MVP, prototype testing was conducted to evaluate whether the proposed solution addressed the problems identified during Iterations 1 and 2.

The goal was to determine whether students understood the concept, whether the proposed features were useful, and which features should be prioritised in the MVP.

---

## Prototype Description

The prototype consisted of digital mock-ups and early interface designs representing the proposed UQ Club Discovery Hub.

The prototype included:

* Homepage concept
* Club browsing page
* Search functionality
* Category filtering
* Club detail pages
* Join club workflow
* User profile concept

The prototype was designed to test the project's value proposition:

> Helping students confidently choose clubs through structured and comparable information.

---

## Testing Method

Prototype testing was conducted with university students representing the target user group.

Participants were asked to:

1. Explore the prototype.
2. Explain what they believed the platform was designed to do.
3. Identify useful and unnecessary features.
4. Describe what information they would need before joining a club.
5. Suggest improvements to the design and functionality.

Feedback was collected through interviews, discussion, and observation.

---

## Key Findings

### Finding 1: Students wanted club information in one place

Participants reported that existing club information was spread across multiple platforms, making comparison difficult.

**Decision:** The MVP should provide structured club information within a single platform.

---

### Finding 2: Search functionality was considered essential

Participants did not want to manually browse large numbers of clubs.

**Decision:** Search functionality was included as a core MVP feature.

---

### Finding 3: Category filtering reduced information overload

Students wanted an easier way to narrow down options based on interests.

**Decision:** Category filtering was implemented in the MVP.

---

### Finding 4: Detailed club information increased confidence

Participants wanted practical information such as activities, contact details, and club descriptions before making decisions.

**Decision:** Club detail modals were included in the MVP.

---

### Finding 5: Users wanted a clear action after browsing

Participants expected a simple next step once they found an interesting club.

**Decision:** A join-club feature was added to the MVP.

---

## Prototype Feedback to MVP Features

| Prototype Feedback                        | MVP Feature Implemented |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Club information is difficult to compare  | Structured club cards   |
| Too many clubs to browse manually         | Search functionality    |
| Students need easier filtering            | Category filters        |
| More information is needed before joining | Club detail modal       |
| Users want a next step after exploration  | Join club interaction   |
| Users want a more complete experience     | Profile page            |

---

## Learning

Prototype testing reinforced a key insight from earlier iterations:

Students already have ways to discover clubs through Market Day, social media, friends, and the UQ Union website.

The main challenge is helping students evaluate and compare clubs with confidence.

This learning directly influenced the design and implementation of the MVP.

---

## Conclusion

Prototype testing provided evidence that students valued structured club information, search functionality, filtering tools, and detailed club profiles.

The feedback collected during testing directly influenced MVP feature decisions and guided the development of the final React-based UQ Club Discovery Hub MVP.
