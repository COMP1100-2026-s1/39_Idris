# Product Testing Summary – Iteration 3 MVP

## Purpose of Product Testing

The purpose of the product-testing interviews was to evaluate whether our working MVP helps UQ students discover, compare and choose clubs more confidently.

Unlike the general interviews, which focused on validating the problem and student behaviour, the product-testing interviews focused on the actual MVP experience. Participants were asked to use the platform and then give feedback on usability, filtering, club profiles, comparison, joining flow, and social proof.

The MVP tested was a basic but functional React application for UQ club discovery. It included:

* club browsing
* category filtering
* club detail pages
* joining clubs
* profile page
* joined clubs display
* social proof / friends activity feature

## Participants

Product testing was conducted with students representing different levels of club experience.

### Participant 1: Emily – First-year international student

Emily had explored UQ clubs before but had not joined any clubs. Her main issue was uncertainty and feeling overwhelmed by scattered information.

After using the MVP, Emily said the platform made club discovery feel more organised and less stressful. She especially found filtering and comparison useful because they helped her move from a long list of clubs to a smaller shortlist.

Emily said the platform increased her confidence from around 4/10 to 7/10 because she could compare clubs and understand them more clearly. However, she still wanted more information about club culture, member experience and whether beginners would feel welcome.

Key learning from Emily:

* Filtering helps reduce overwhelm.
* Comparison improves decision confidence.
* Club profiles are useful but do not fully communicate club culture.
* Student reviews or testimonials could reduce uncertainty further.

### Participant 2: Jack – Experienced club member

Jack is a second-year student who joined several clubs in first year and is now active in one club. His main issue was not finding clubs, but evaluating whether clubs are genuinely worth joining.

After testing the MVP, Jack said the comparison feature was the most useful part because students already compare clubs manually through multiple tabs, Instagram pages and websites. He felt the platform saved time and made differences between similar clubs easier to understand.

Jack also responded positively to the Social Proof / Friends Activity feature. He said friend recommendations are still more powerful than platform information alone because friends provide context about the real club experience. However, he thought the feature was useful because it gave students another confidence signal when deciding which clubs to explore.

Key learning from Jack:

* The MVP helps students evaluate clubs more efficiently.
* Comparison is useful for experienced students as well as first-year students.
* Social proof supports trust but should be strengthened with real student reviews.
* The platform helps students shortlist clubs rather than fully replacing real-life recommendations.

### Participant 3: Sarah – Third-year low-engagement student

Sarah joined several clubs in first year but rarely attends club events now because she balances study, work and personal commitments. Her main concern was whether clubs are worth her limited time.

After testing the MVP, Sarah said the platform was useful because it helped her rule out irrelevant clubs quickly. She said this was valuable because busy students do not only need to find clubs; they need to decide which clubs deserve their attention.

Sarah liked that the MVP made information easier to process. However, she still wanted stronger evidence of value, such as member outcomes, event attendance, reviews or examples of what students actually gained from joining.

Key learning from Sarah:

* The MVP can help busy students save time.
* Filtering and club profiles help students quickly remove unsuitable options.
* Students need evidence that a club is worth the time commitment.
* Social proof and student-generated content would make the MVP more persuasive.

## Summary of Findings

Overall, product testing showed that the MVP provides value as a basic functional club discovery platform.

The strongest validated features were:

* filtering
* club profiles
* comparison
* profile-based joined clubs
* social proof / friends activity

Participants agreed that the MVP was more organised than searching across multiple platforms such as Instagram, UQ pages, Discord or Facebook. They also said that the platform helped reduce confusion by bringing club information into one place.

However, the testing also showed that basic information alone is not enough. Students still want more authentic evidence about the real club experience.

Common feedback across participants:

* Students want to compare clubs more easily.
* Students want to understand what clubs are actually like.
* Friend recommendations and student reviews increase trust.
* Social proof helps reduce uncertainty but should be expanded.
* The MVP is useful even if it is not polished, because it provides a working structure for club discovery and decision-making.

## Connection to MVP Hypotheses

### Discovery & Overwhelm

Product testing supported this hypothesis. Participants said the MVP reduced the effort required to search for clubs because club information was centralised and filters helped narrow down options.

### Decision Uncertainty

This hypothesis was strongly supported. Participants said the MVP helped them feel more informed and more confident, especially when comparing clubs. However, they still wanted more student reviews, testimonials and club culture information.

### Joining Friction

This hypothesis was partially supported. Participants did not see the joining process itself as the biggest problem. Instead, they said uncertainty before joining makes even small barriers feel more difficult. This suggests the MVP should focus on increasing confidence before students reach the join stage.

### Social Influence

This hypothesis was partially supported. Participants said friend activity, peer recommendations and social proof made clubs feel more trustworthy. However, they also said the feature would be stronger with real student-generated content such as reviews, ratings and member testimonials.

## Decisions for Future Iterations

Based on the product testing, we decided to keep the current MVP direction and improve it by focusing on decision confidence rather than only discovery.

Future improvements could include:

* student reviews
* ratings
* member testimonials
* “what to expect at your first event” comments
* activity level indicators
* clearer beginner-friendly labels
* more detailed club culture information
* improved comparison between similar clubs

## Final Product Testing Conclusion

The MVP does not completely solve every problem in the club-joining process, but it successfully addresses the key issue identified in earlier iterations: students do not simply struggle to find clubs; they struggle to choose clubs confidently.

The working MVP helps students browse, filter, compare and evaluate clubs more efficiently. The Social Proof / Friends Activity feature also supports trust and confidence by showing peer activity and recommendations.

Therefore, the MVP is a valid functional attempt that connects directly to our problem evidence, solution testing, prototype feedback and Iteration 3 product-testing results.
