

General Interview Transcript


Participant: UQ student
Context: Before using the website prototype

⸻

Interviewer:
Hi, thank you for doing this interview with me. Before we start, I just want to say there are no right or wrong answers. I’m mainly trying to understand how students usually find and decide whether to join clubs. So you can just speak honestly from your own experience.

Participant:
Yeah, no worries.

Interviewer:
To start with, have you joined any UQ clubs before?

Participant:
Yeah, I have joined a few before, but not that many. I think I joined one cultural club and one academic club before. But honestly, I also looked at a lot of clubs and then didn’t end up joining them.

Interviewer:
Why did you decide not to join some of them?

Participant:
I think sometimes I was interested at first, but then I couldn’t really tell what the club was actually like. Like, the club name sounded interesting, or their Instagram looked nice, but I still didn’t know what people actually do in the club. So I just didn’t feel confident enough to join.

Interviewer:
So it wasn’t that you couldn’t find clubs at all, but more that you weren’t sure which one was right for you?

Participant:
Yeah, exactly. I think finding clubs is not the hardest part. There are club lists, market day, Instagram, and friends talking about clubs. But choosing one is harder. Because there are many options, and a lot of them sound similar.

Interviewer:
Can you walk me through how you usually search for clubs?

Participant:
Usually at the beginning of the semester, I might see clubs at Market Day. I also sometimes check Instagram if someone shares a post. Sometimes I go to the UQ Union website, but I don’t always use it deeply. And sometimes I just hear about clubs from friends.

Interviewer:
When you use those different sources, does it feel easy or confusing?

Participant:
A bit confusing, to be honest. The information is kind of everywhere. Like Instagram has photos and event posts, but it’s not always clear what the club is really about. The official website might have a list, but sometimes the information feels too short or not updated. And friends can recommend clubs, but that only depends on what they know.

Interviewer:
What part feels most confusing when you search?

Participant:
I think the hardest part is comparing clubs. For example, if I’m interested in two or three clubs, I have to open different pages and remember all the details myself. One club might have information on Instagram, another one might have a form link, another one might have Discord. It’s just a bit messy.

Interviewer:
Do you ever feel overwhelmed by too many club options?

Participant:
Yeah, definitely. Especially at Market Day. There are so many stalls, and everyone is trying to explain their club. It’s exciting, but also overwhelming. After a while, I feel like I don’t remember which club is which.

Interviewer:
That makes sense. When there are too many options, how do you decide which clubs to focus on?

Participant:
Usually I just focus on clubs that my friends mention, or clubs that have nice photos or clear information. Sometimes I also choose based on whether the club looks welcoming. But I don’t have a very systematic way to compare them. It’s more like a feeling.

Interviewer:
What kind of information do you usually look for before deciding whether to join?

Participant:
I want to know what events they run, how often they meet, whether I need experience, how much it costs, and whether it’s okay to join alone. I also want to know what the people are like. Like, is it casual? Is it very serious? Is it mostly for people who already know each other?

Interviewer:
Is that information usually easy to find?

Participant:
Not really. Some clubs explain it well, but many don’t. A lot of club pages just say something like “we are a friendly community” or “we organise fun events,” but that doesn’t really tell me much. I want more specific information.

Interviewer:
What do you mean by specific information?

Participant:
Like examples. If they say they have social events, what kind of social events? Dinner? Games? Networking? Workshops? And how often? Once a week or once a month? Also, if I’m new, can I just show up, or do I need to know someone first? These details matter.

Interviewer:
Have you ever wanted to join a club but then changed your mind?

Participant:
Yeah, I have. Sometimes I was interested, but I didn’t know what the first step was. Like, do I sign up online? Do I pay first? Do I message someone? Do I wait for the next event? If it feels too unclear, I might just leave it.

Interviewer:
So the joining process itself can stop you?

Participant:
Yeah, for sure. I think if the joining process is simple and clear, I’m more likely to do it. But if I have to search around for links or ask someone, sometimes I just don’t bother, especially if I’m already busy.

Interviewer:
What would an ideal joining process look like to you?

Participant:
Probably something very step-by-step. Like, step one: read the club information. Step two: fill in this form. Step three: pay the membership fee if needed. Step four: join the group chat or come to the next event. I just want to know exactly what to do.

Interviewer:
Do you think uncertainty is a big reason why students hesitate to join clubs?

Participant:
Yes, I think so. For me, uncertainty is a big thing. I might think a club looks interesting, but I’m not sure if I’ll fit in. I don’t know if the people are friendly, if the events are awkward, or if everyone already has friends there.

Interviewer:
What would make you feel more confident before joining?

Participant:
Real student comments would help. Like if current members say what their experience was like. Photos from actual events would also help. And clear tags like “beginner-friendly,” “low commitment,” or “good for first-year students” would make it easier.

Interviewer:
Would you trust student reviews?

Participant:
Yeah, I think I would, especially if they are verified students. I don’t need super long reviews. Even short comments would help because they feel more real than official club descriptions.

Interviewer:
Would negative or mixed reviews put you off?

Participant:
Not always. Actually, I think mixed reviews can be helpful. If everything is only positive, it feels like advertising. But if someone says, “The club is fun, but events can be crowded,” that gives me a more realistic idea. Then I can decide if I’m okay with that.

Interviewer:
How important is club culture to you?

Participant:
Very important. I think club culture is probably one of the biggest things. A club can have a topic I like, but if the atmosphere doesn’t suit me, I probably won’t stay. For example, some clubs are very outgoing and loud, which is good for some people, but maybe not for everyone. Some clubs are more relaxed. I want to know that before joining.

Interviewer:
Do you usually know the club culture before you join?

Participant:
Not really. Usually I only find out after going to an event. But that can be scary because going to the first event already takes effort. If I feel unsure, I might not go at all.

Interviewer:
Have friends influenced your decision to join a club before?

Participant:
Yes, definitely. If a friend joins, I feel more comfortable. It’s less scary to go with someone. Also, if a friend says a club is good, I trust that more than just seeing a post online.

Interviewer:
Would you say friends are the main reason you join clubs?

Participant:
Sometimes, but not always. Friends make me more likely to try a club, but I still care about whether I actually like the club. If my friend joins a club that doesn’t fit me, I probably still won’t continue.

Interviewer:
So social influence helps, but it doesn’t fully decide for you?

Participant:
Yeah, exactly. It helps me notice clubs and feel less nervous, but I still need enough information to decide.

Interviewer:
When you compare clubs, what factors matter most to you?

Participant:
Probably interest, time commitment, cost, event type, and whether it feels welcoming. Also, I care about whether I can join casually or if I have to commit a lot. Some clubs are fun but require regular attendance, and I don’t always have time for that.

Interviewer:
Do you think students often compare clubs side by side?

Participant:
Maybe mentally, but not in a proper way. Like I might think, “This club seems fun, but this other club seems more useful.” But I don’t write it down or compare properly. It’s just in my head, so I forget details easily.

Interviewer:
Would a tool that lets students compare clubs side by side be useful?

Participant:
Yeah, I think that would be very useful. Especially if it compares things like cost, event frequency, commitment, beginner-friendliness, and joining steps. That would save time.

Interviewer:
What problems do you think first-year students face when choosing clubs?

Participant:
I think first-year students probably feel even more confused because they don’t know the university environment yet. They might not know where to find club information, and they might feel nervous about going alone. International students might also have the same issue, maybe even more, because they may not know many people yet.

Interviewer:
If you had a website for choosing clubs, what would you expect it to include?

Participant:
I would expect search and filters first. Then clear club profiles. I would also want student reviews, photos, event examples, costs, and joining steps. Maybe also tags so I can quickly see if a club is beginner-friendly or low commitment.

Interviewer:
What would make you stop using that kind of website?

Participant:
If the information is outdated or too vague, I would stop using it. Also, if it just feels like another club list without useful details, then it’s not that different from existing pages. The value would be in making the information clearer and easier to compare.

Interviewer:
That’s really helpful. So from your perspective, the main problem is not just discovery, but confidence in choosing?

Participant:
Yeah, I think that’s right. I can discover clubs, but I don’t always feel confident choosing one. I need to know what the experience is actually like.

Interviewer:
If you could ask every club to provide three pieces of information, what would they be?

Participant:
First, what their events are actually like. Second, how much time and money it takes. Third, whether new students or people coming alone are welcome. Those three things would help a lot.

Interviewer:
That’s a good summary. Before we finish, is there anything else you want to add?

Participant:
Maybe just that students don’t always want more information, they want clearer information. Sometimes there is already information online, but it’s scattered or too promotional. I think if there was one place that made clubs easier to understand and compare, I would use it.

Interviewer:
Thank you so much. That was really helpful.

Participant:
No worries. I’m glad I could help.
