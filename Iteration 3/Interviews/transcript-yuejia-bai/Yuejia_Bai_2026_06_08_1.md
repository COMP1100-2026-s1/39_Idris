**Duration:** Around 12 minutes
**Participant:** UQ student
**Context:** The participant has just used the club selection website prototype.

---

**Interviewer:**
Thank you for testing our website. Before we start, I just want to say there are no right or wrong answers. We are mainly trying to understand how the website feels from a student’s perspective. Could you briefly tell me what you did when you first opened the website?

**Participant:**
Yeah, sure. When I first opened it, I looked at the homepage and tried to understand what the website was for. I could tell quite quickly that it was about helping students find or compare clubs. Then I used the search and filter section first, because that felt like the most direct way to start looking for a club.

**Interviewer:**
When you used the search or filter function, what was your first impression?

**Participant:**
I think it was useful because it gave me a more structured way to search. Normally, if I look for clubs, I might go on Instagram, the UQ Union website, or ask friends. But the information is usually scattered. Here, having filters made it feel more organised. I could narrow down clubs based on interest or category, which made the process feel less overwhelming.

**Interviewer:**
Did anything feel confusing when you were using the filters?

**Participant:**
A little bit. I understood the basic idea, but I think some filter labels could be clearer. For example, if there is a category like “social” or “professional,” sometimes clubs can fit into both. So I was not always sure which filter I should choose. Maybe it would help if the website allowed multiple categories, or explained what each category means.

**Interviewer:**
That makes sense. Did the filtering experience help you find clubs more easily compared with how you normally search?

**Participant:**
Yes, definitely. I think it helped reduce the number of choices. Usually there are so many clubs, and I don’t know where to start. With the filter, I could at least remove clubs that were not relevant to me. I think that makes the decision process faster.

**Interviewer:**
After filtering, you opened some club profiles. What did you think about the club profile pages?

**Participant:**
I liked that the information was in one place. The profile felt more useful than just a short Instagram bio. I could see what the club does, who it is suitable for, and maybe what kind of activities they run. That kind of information is important because I don’t just want to know that a club exists. I want to know what it actually feels like to be part of it.

**Interviewer:**
Was there any information on the club profile that you found especially helpful?

**Participant:**
I think the most helpful part was the section explaining what students can expect from the club. For example, whether it is casual, competitive, beginner-friendly, or more commitment-heavy. I also liked if there was information about event frequency, because sometimes I hesitate to join a club if I don’t know how much time it will take.

**Interviewer:**
Was there anything missing from the club profile?

**Participant:**
Maybe more real student experiences. Like short reviews or comments from current members. I don’t mean long reviews, but just small honest comments. For example, “I joined as a first-year student and it was easy to meet people,” or “Most events are on weekday evenings.” That would help me imagine the real experience better.

**Interviewer:**
So would you say student reviews could make the club feel more trustworthy?

**Participant:**
Yes, I think so. Club descriptions can sometimes sound promotional because they are written by the club itself. But student reviews feel more honest. Even if the review is not perfect, it helps me understand what the club is actually like. It would make me feel more confident before joining.

**Interviewer:**
You also tried the comparison feature. How did you feel about comparing clubs side by side?

**Participant:**
I found that very useful. I think comparison is one of the strongest parts of the website. When clubs are listed separately, I have to remember the details myself, which is hard. But if I can compare them side by side, I can see differences more clearly, like event frequency, cost, commitment level, and club culture.

**Interviewer:**
Did the comparison feature change how you would make a decision?

**Participant:**
Yes. I think it would help me narrow down my final choice. For example, if two clubs look similar, the comparison table can show which one is more beginner-friendly or which one has more social events. Without that, I might just choose randomly or give up.

**Interviewer:**
Was there anything about the comparison flow that could be improved?

**Participant:**
Maybe the website could make it clearer how to add clubs to comparison. I figured it out after using it for a while, but at first I was not completely sure. Maybe a button like “Add to compare” should stand out more. Also, if I select several clubs, it would be helpful to have a small reminder showing which clubs I already selected.

**Interviewer:**
That is helpful feedback. When you were using the website, did you feel overwhelmed at any point?

**Participant:**
Not really overwhelmed, but I did feel that if there were too many clubs shown at once, it might become a bit much. The filter helped with that, but I think the homepage or search results should avoid showing too much information at the same time. A clean layout is important.

**Interviewer:**
Compared with your normal club-searching process, did this website reduce uncertainty for you?

**Participant:**
Yes, I think it did. The main uncertainty for me is not just finding clubs. It is knowing whether I will actually fit in. This website helps because it gives more details about club culture, activities, and commitment. So I would feel more comfortable choosing one.

**Interviewer:**
What kind of information would make you feel almost 100% confident that a club is a good fit?

**Participant:**
I would want to know what the events are actually like, how often they happen, whether new members are welcomed, and whether I need prior experience. Also, photos or examples of past events would help. Student comments would also be important. If I can see that other students had a positive experience, I would feel more confident.

**Interviewer:**
You also looked at the joining section. How did the joining flow feel?

**Participant:**
It was pretty straightforward. I liked that the website made the next step clear. Sometimes clubs say “join us” but then you have to find a form, message them on Instagram, or wait for a market day. Here, if the joining steps are clearly listed, it feels easier.

**Interviewer:**
Was there anything annoying or inconvenient about the joining process?

**Participant:**
I think it depends on how many steps there are. If I have to leave the website and go to another platform, it might feel a bit less smooth. But even if that happens, it is okay as long as the website clearly tells me what to do. For example, “Step 1: fill out this form. Step 2: pay membership fee. Step 3: join Discord.” That would be very helpful.

**Interviewer:**
Did the website make you more likely to join a club?

**Participant:**
Yes, I think so. If I was already interested in clubs, this website would make it easier to take action. The biggest reason is that it reduces the effort of searching and comparing. If I feel less confused, I am more likely to actually join.

**Interviewer:**
What about social features? For example, seeing friend activity, recommendations, or popular clubs. Would that influence you?

**Participant:**
Yes, but maybe as a supporting feature, not the main feature. If I see that my friends joined a club, I might be more interested because it feels less scary. But I still want real information about the club. I would not join only because it is popular. Popularity helps me notice a club, but detailed information helps me decide.

**Interviewer:**
That is a good distinction. Would recommendations make the website feel more useful?

**Participant:**
Yes, if the recommendations are based on my interests. For example, if I select that I am interested in sports, volunteering, or career development, then the website recommends clubs that match those interests. That would save time. But I would want to know why a club is recommended, not just see a random list.

**Interviewer:**
During the testing, were there any parts of the website where you felt unsure what to click?

**Participant:**
Maybe only the comparison part at the beginning. I think most of the website was understandable. But some buttons could be more obvious. Also, the website should make the user journey clear: search first, open profile, compare, then join. If that flow is visually clear, it would be easier for new users.

**Interviewer:**
If you could change one thing about the website, what would it be?

**Participant:**
I would improve the club profile pages by adding more real student perspective. I think the structure is already useful, but the website would be stronger if it included verified student reviews, common questions, or examples of past activities. That would make the information feel more trustworthy.

**Interviewer:**
And what is one thing you think we should definitely keep?

**Participant:**
Definitely the comparison feature. I think that is the most useful part because it directly solves the problem of choosing between clubs. Searching is useful, but comparison helps with the final decision. I would also keep the clear club profile structure.

**Interviewer:**
Overall, how would you describe your experience using the website?

**Participant:**
Overall, it was positive. I think the website addresses a real problem. Students can already find clubs in different places, but it is hard to compare them and know which one is right. This website makes the process feel more organised and less stressful. I think it would be especially useful for first-year students or international students who may not know where to start.

**Interviewer:**
Would you personally use this website if it became available?

**Participant:**
Yes, I would. Especially at the beginning of semester, when I am deciding what clubs to join. I would use it to explore options first, compare a few clubs, and then decide which ones I want to join or follow.

**Interviewer:**
Before we finish, is there anything else you want to add?

**Participant:**
Maybe just that the website should avoid feeling too promotional. I think students trust information more when it feels balanced. So instead of only saying every club is amazing, it should show practical details, like time commitment, cost, beginner-friendliness, and real student feedback. That would make the website more useful and trustworthy.

**Interviewer:**
Thank you so much. Your feedback is really helpful. It gives us a better idea of what works well and what still needs improvement.

**Participant:**
No problem. I think the idea is useful, and I hope the website keeps improving.
