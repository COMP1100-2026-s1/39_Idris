Learning Card 1: Students need practical details, not only promotional descriptions

Linked test

Test Card 1: Clear club information

What we thought

We thought that showing club names, categories, and short descriptions would be enough for students to understand whether a club matched their interests.

What happened

Participants liked the structured information, but they still wanted more practical details. They cared about things such as meeting time, cost, location, event frequency, and what the club is actually like.

What we learned

Students do not only need to discover clubs. They need enough trustworthy information to compare clubs and choose one with confidence.

MVP decision

We should keep the structured club cards and detail modal, but future versions should include more verified club information and possibly student reviews.

Learning Card 2: Filters reduce browsing effort

Linked test

Test Card 2: Search and filtering

What we thought

We thought search and category filters would make the club browsing process easier.

What happened

Participants were able to use search and filtering to narrow down the club list. This made the platform feel easier to use than checking many separate social media pages or scrolling through a long list.

What we learned

Filtering is not just a small extra feature. It is important because students often already have a rough interest area in mind.

MVP decision

Search and category filters should stay as core MVP features. They directly support the value proposition of helping students compare clubs more efficiently.

Learning Card 3: The profile page needs more useful feedback

Linked test

Test Card 3: Join and profile feature

What we thought

We thought that showing joined clubs on the profile page would be enough to make the user flow feel complete.

What happened

The join and profile feature made the MVP feel more like a working product, but the profile page was still very simple. It could do more to help students reflect on their club choices.

What we learned

A profile page should not only store joined clubs. It can also help students understand their interests and make better future choices.

MVP decision

A Club Preference Summary was added to the profile page. This gives users a short summary based on the clubs they have joined and makes the profile page more useful.
