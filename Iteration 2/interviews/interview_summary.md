# Interview Summary  
**Iteration 2 – UQ Club Discovery Platform**

## Overview
This summary combines the interview findings from all 5 team members.  
Each person completed 5 interviews, so the total sample size is **25 users**.

Overall, the interviews were quite consistent. Most users were interested in clubs, but they often felt unsure when it came to actually choosing one. The main issue was usually not discovering that clubs exist, but understanding what each club is really like and whether it would suit them.

---

## H1 – Filters: Partial Support
Most users said filters would be helpful, especially **interest** and **time commitment**. These were usually the first things people wanted to use when searching for clubs.

At the same time, filters did not fully solve the problem. They helped users narrow down the options, but they usually did **not** help them make the final decision.

A lot of users also wanted more practical filters, such as:
- location
- meeting time
- membership fee
- activity level
- atmosphere
- online/offline option
- type of opportunity or experience

### What we learned
Filters are useful, but only as a first step. They help reduce the number of choices, but users still need much clearer information before they feel confident enough to choose a club.

---

## H2 – Social Features: Partial Support
Social features were generally seen as helpful, especially at the start of the process. Many users said they would be more likely to check out a club if they saw that their friends were interested in it or had already joined.

However, this was usually not enough to make them join or stay long-term. Social influence helped reduce awkwardness and increased curiosity, but it was not the main reason for commitment.

Some users also mentioned privacy concerns, especially if too much activity was shown automatically.

### What we learned
Social features can help users feel more comfortable exploring clubs, but they work more as support than as the main decision factor.

---

## H3 – Clear Club Information: Strongly Supported
This was the strongest finding across all interview summaries.

Again and again, users said the biggest issue was that they could not clearly understand:
- what the club actually does
- what the real experience is like
- whether it is beginner-friendly
- whether it matches their interests, schedule, and expectations

The most commonly requested information included:
- real activities
- meeting times and event frequency
- membership fees
- club culture or atmosphere
- recent activity
- photos
- examples of events
- short member comments or reviews

A lot of current club information was described as:
- vague
- outdated
- incomplete
- too general or too promotional

### What we learned
The biggest pain point is not finding clubs, but understanding them clearly enough to make a confident decision. Clear and realistic club information seems to matter more than any other feature we tested.

---

## H4 – Easy Joining Process: Supported, but Conditional
Most users liked the idea of a simple and fast joining process. They said this would make them more likely to take action while they were still interested.

Still, the interviews also showed that a simple process only helps **after** users already understand and trust the club. If the information is still unclear, then even an easy joining flow does not fully solve the problem.

Some users also preferred softer first steps, such as:
- save
- follow
- register interest
- attend one event first before fully joining

### What we learned
A simple joining process is important, but it is not enough on its own. It works best when the club information is already clear and trustworthy.

---

## Common Pain Points
Across the interviews, the same problems appeared repeatedly:

- club information is unclear or spread across different places
- users cannot picture what joining the club would actually be like
- filters help search, but not final decisions
- social features help first interest, but not long-term commitment
- joining can feel confusing if the next steps are unclear
- lack of schedule, fees, and real examples creates uncertainty

---

## Opportunities
From these interviews, the main opportunities seem to be:

- smarter and more practical filters
- clearer and more structured club profiles
- better presentation of real activities, recent events, and club atmosphere
- a simple and transparent joining process
- optional social proof features
- save / compare / register-interest options
- timetable integration or clearer schedule visibility

---

## Key Takeaway
The main takeaway from the interviews is that students do not mainly struggle with **discovering** clubs.  
They struggle with **choosing confidently**.

Filters help them narrow down choices.  
Social features help them feel more comfortable exploring.  
A simple joining process helps them act.  
But the biggest factor in whether they actually decide to join is **clear, realistic, and trustworthy information about the club**.

---

## Conclusion
Overall, these interviews suggest that our MVP should focus less on just helping users find clubs faster, and more on helping them understand clubs better.

If users can clearly see what a club does, what the atmosphere is like, and what to expect, they are much more likely to make a decision. Filters, social features, and joining flow still matter, but they seem to work best as supporting features around strong club information.
