# Iteration 2 – UQ Club Discovery Platform

This folder contains our team’s Iteration 2 work for the **UQ Club Discovery Platform** project.

The main goal of this iteration was to test our key assumptions about how UQ students discover, compare, and join clubs, and then use that evidence to refine our customer understanding, value proposition, and product direction.

## Main Insight

Our strongest finding from this iteration is:

**Students can already discover clubs, but they cannot confidently choose one.**

This means the main problem is not just discovery.  
The bigger issue is **decision uncertainty** — students often cannot clearly understand what a club is really like, whether it fits their schedule, or whether it is the right choice for them.

Because of this, our product direction is moving from a basic **club discovery tool** to a **decision-support platform**.

## Main Customer Focus

Our primary customer segment in this iteration is:

- **First-year and newly enrolled UQ students**

These students are usually interested in joining clubs, but often feel uncertain when comparing options, understanding expectations, and deciding whether a club is a good fit.

We also considered:
- returning students
- postgraduate students
- club organisers (secondary)

## What We Tested

This iteration focused on 4 main hypotheses:

1. **Smart Discovery (Filters)**  
   Whether filters help students find suitable clubs faster.

2. **Social-driven Features**  
   Whether seeing friend activity makes students more willing to explore or join clubs.

3. **Clear Club Information**  
   Whether clear and structured club information helps students decide faster and with more confidence.

4. **Easy Joining Process**  
   Whether a simple joining process reduces friction and increases willingness to join.

## What We Learned

From interviews and analysis, we learned that:

- filters help narrow options, but do not solve the final decision problem
- social features can reduce anxiety and increase curiosity, but are not the main reason students join
- clear, realistic, and trustworthy club information is the strongest user need
- a simple joining process only works after users already trust the club information

This shifted our focus from **improving discovery only** to **supporting confident decision-making through clarity and trust**.
