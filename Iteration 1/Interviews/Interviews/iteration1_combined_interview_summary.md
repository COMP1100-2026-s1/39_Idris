# Iteration 1 – Combined Interview Summary  
## UQ Club Discovery and Participation

### Introduction
This document presents the combined findings from **25 student interviews** conducted for Iteration 1. The purpose of these interviews was to better understand students’ awareness of UQ clubs, their experience of discovering and comparing clubs, the role of social influence in participation, and the key barriers that prevent students from joining.

The interview results reveal that while most students are aware that UQ offers a wide range of clubs and societies, many experience significant difficulty when trying to move from initial awareness to actual participation.

---

## Overall Results

| Category | Result |
|----------|--------|
| **Awareness** | **24/25** |
| **Discovery** | **21/25** |
| **Social Influence** | **22/25** |
| **Barriers** | **23/25** |

---

## 1. Awareness (24/25)

The interviews show that most students are aware of the **general existence** of UQ clubs and societies. Students commonly mentioned broad categories such as sports clubs, cultural societies, volunteering groups, and degree-related clubs. However, although general awareness was high, knowledge of **specific clubs** was often limited.

Students most frequently learned about clubs through:

- **Market Day / O-Week**
- **Social media**, especially Instagram
- **Friends and peer networks**
- **Emails and course-related exposure**

A common pattern across the interviews was that awareness was strongest during highly visible campus events, particularly Market Day, but tended to decline afterwards. This suggests that initial exposure is effective, but ongoing visibility is much weaker.

**Key finding:** students usually know that clubs exist, but many do not have a clear or lasting understanding of what specific clubs are available.

---

## 2. Discovery (21/25)

Discovery emerged as one of the strongest pain points across the interviews. Most students reported that it was difficult to **find**, **compare**, and **evaluate** clubs in a clear and efficient way. Information was often described as:

- scattered across multiple platforms
- difficult to verify
- inconsistent or outdated
- lacking enough detail to support decision-making

Many students explained that searching for clubs felt effortful because they often had to move between Instagram, Google, official UQ pages, and club-specific pages without finding a centralised and reliable source of information.

However, one interview set showed a different result, indicating that discovery felt relatively easy during **Market Day**, when clubs were physically visible and students could interact with them directly. This is an important contrast: discovery appears to work well in **event-based environments**, but much less effectively in normal day-to-day searching.

**Key finding:** discovery is not consistently effective. It works best when clubs are highly visible and interactive, but becomes much more difficult outside those contexts.

---

## 3. Social Influence (22/25)

Social influence was another major theme. Across the interviews, friends were shown to play an important role in whether students actually chose to participate in a club. Many students said that they felt more comfortable joining when:

- a friend was already involved
- they could attend with someone they knew
- the social risk of joining alone was reduced

For many students, friends did not necessarily create the original interest in a club, but they often acted as the factor that transformed **interest into action**. In other words, social influence reduced awkwardness, uncertainty, and hesitation.

At the same time, a small number of students were more willing to join independently if the club environment appeared welcoming or clearly aligned with their interests. This suggests that while social influence is highly important, it is not equally strong for every student.

**Key finding:** friends are not always the sole reason students join, but they are a powerful factor in reducing hesitation and encouraging participation.

---

## 4. Barriers (23/25)

The interviews identified several recurring barriers that prevented or delayed participation. The most common barriers included:

- **unclear or incomplete information**
- **outdated or hard-to-find details**
- **uncertainty about what to expect**
- **difficulty understanding how to join**
- **time conflicts with study and other commitments**
- **concerns about overcommitment**
- **social discomfort or nervousness**
- **the effort required to find reliable information**

A particularly important insight is that the main issue was **not a lack of student interest**. In many cases, students were interested in clubs, but did not follow through because the process felt unclear, effortful, or socially uncertain. This suggests that the barrier is often one of **friction**, rather than motivation alone.

**Key finding:** most barriers relate to uncertainty, effort, and lack of clarity, rather than to a complete absence of interest.

---

## 5. Major Patterns Across the Interviews

Several broader patterns emerged when the results were considered together:

### 5.1 Awareness is broad but shallow
Students generally know that clubs exist, but often cannot name many specific clubs or describe them in detail.

### 5.2 Discovery is the main structural weakness
Students struggle to move from “I know clubs exist” to “I know which club suits me and how to join it.”

### 5.3 Social comfort matters
For many students, friends reduce the emotional and social risk of participation.

### 5.4 Interest does not automatically lead to action
Students may be curious about clubs, but unclear information and effortful processes often prevent participation.

### 5.5 Event-based visibility is more effective than passive online searching
Market Day was repeatedly described as one of the clearest and easiest ways for students to discover clubs.

---

## 6. Implications

These findings suggest that the current challenge is not simply promoting the existence of clubs, but improving the **pathway from awareness to participation**. Students need clearer, more centralised, and more trustworthy information in order to make decisions confidently.

Possible improvements suggested across the interviews included:

- a **centralised platform** for all clubs
- better **filtering and categorisation**
- clearer information about **activities, commitment, and joining steps**
- more visible indications of whether a club is **active and beginner-friendly**
- personalised recommendations based on student interests
- social features that show whether friends are interested or involved

---

## Conclusion

In conclusion, the combined interview findings show that UQ students are generally aware of clubs, but many experience difficulty when trying to discover, compare, and join them. Social influence plays an important role in reducing hesitation, while barriers such as unclear information, uncertainty, and time-related concerns frequently prevent participation.

Overall, the evidence suggests that improving the accessibility, clarity, and usability of club information could significantly improve student engagement. The central issue is not that students lack interest, but that the current discovery and joining process often creates too much friction for that interest to become action.
