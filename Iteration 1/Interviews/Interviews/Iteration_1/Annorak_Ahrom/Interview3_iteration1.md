Speaker 2:

Are there any clubs that you were interested in but didn't join?

Yeah — UQ Space and UQ Racing. I think they're just a bit hard to get into in first year. I feel like it's quite limited in numbers, and in second or third year it will be more achievable to get into.

Speaker 1:
Why is it hard for first years?

Speaker 2:
I think they're looking for a bit of experience, and because the teams take part in big events, they're obviously looking for more experienced people. Maybe networking would be the best way to get in.

Speaker 1:
What kind of events?

Speaker 2:
For UQ Racing, they participate in national and even international competitions. For UQ Space and UQ Racing, they compete against other universities in Queensland. It's about representing your uni at a national or state level.

Speaker 1:
How did you feel not being able to join these clubs?

Speaker 2:
I felt fine because I kind of knew it wasn’t really a first-year thing. It’s just something I’m interested in. It was still good to see them at Market Day, and I’ll definitely consider joining next year.

Speaker 1:
Do you think it should be a first-year thing?

Speaker 2:
To be honest, I can see why they wouldn’t take first years, but I think first years being allowed to at least go to some of the events would be good. I haven’t done too much research into that, but I’m pretty sure you can’t.

Speaker 1:
Can you tell me about anything positive or negative about your club experiences so far?

Speaker 2:
To be honest, I haven’t had that many club experiences yet, but I think the good thing is that through social media they inform you about upcoming events and what to look out for. It makes things clear, so you don’t really miss events if you follow them.

Speaker 1:
Do you think social media is enough for getting information across?

Speaker 2:
I think it is, but sometimes you still miss an event here and there, and you hear about it through other people. But overall, I think they’re doing a good job.

Speaker 1:
Do you think there’s a better way to do this?

Speaker 2:
Maybe displaying it on TVs around campus. I’m sure they might already do it, but I just haven’t seen it.

Speaker 1:
Would you be more likely to join a club if your friends were also joining?

Speaker 2:
Yeah. I think it’s always good to know someone initially, and it’s easier to make new friends when you already know someone. I just find that easier.

Speaker 1:
Would you join a club alone?

Speaker 2:
I wouldn’t join a club alone, but I haven’t joined any clubs alone yet.

Speaker 1:
Do you think it’s difficult for students to look for clubs?

Speaker 2:
No, it’s not difficult. There are a lot of clubs at UQ, and picking one that suits your interests or hobbies is quite simple.

Speaker 1:
For students who struggle with social events or social anxiety, what do you think could help?

Speaker 2:
I wouldn’t say I’m very non-social, but I am quite introverted. That’s why I like going to clubs where I know at least one other person — it makes it easier to make new friends. But with welcome parties and things like that, you can still meet new people. And if you're a first-year student like me, a lot of people are in the same situation, so it actually makes it easier to adapt.

Speaker 1:
Can you talk about when you first heard about clubs?

Speaker 2:
I actually heard about clubs last year because both my parents work at UQ, and they told me that joining clubs would be good for making new friends, networking, and following your hobbies and interests.

Speaker 1:
Which club caught your attention first?

Speaker 2:
Probably EUS — the Engineering Undergrad Society.

Speaker 1:
For networking purposes?

Speaker 2:
Yeah, and also just to meet new people. It’s good to be associated with people who have similar interests and future career paths.

Nothing specific, but I think finding the right balance in how many clubs you join is important. I was interested in about six clubs, but I ended up joining three because I knew six would be too much.

Speaker 2:
Yeah, so far — in this limited time — it’s been quite easy.
